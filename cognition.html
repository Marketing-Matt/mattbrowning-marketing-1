What I learned at Rackspace, and still believe

Educate before you co-market

I didn't market to AWS. I marketed through their sellers.

The route to market was AWS Marketplace. The thing that actually moved it was internal. I ran an education programme to get our own engineers certified on AWS, and pushed it hard enough that we ended up holding more AWS certifications than AWS's own UK solution engineering team. After that, when an AWS seller needed a partner, we were the easy choice.

Events were split by audience on purpose. Regional Summits for infrastructure teams. re:Invent for leadership. At re:Invent we put 25 CTOs in an executive suite at the Encore with the EMEA GVP of AWS presenting to them, outside the official agenda. No other vendor had that.

The funding followed. $1.2m of AWS market development funds into EMEA inside six months — the largest MDF allocation to any single partner in EMEA at the time. We won it because the plan was specific. Most partners submit something generic.

Same applies to Devin. A partner's delivery engineers decide whether it reaches a client. Their alliance team only decides whether to sign.

And I package it so other people can run it

Starburst · DigiCert

At Starburst I built partner bundles. Packaged co-marketing the partner team could take out and sell, with success criteria agreed before anyone spent money. Partner marketing stopped depending on me being in the room.

That's how Dell ended up on our booth and in the keynote at Big Data LDN. Our budget didn't buy that platform. The bundle did. Dell wrote it up.

Same instinct applied to events at DigiCert. A two-city summit — 205 attendees, $450k — is now a nine-city roadshow. 360 attendees, show rate up from 71% to 90%, NPS 87, on $140k.

Regional marketing scales on things other people can pick up and run. Not on how hard the person running it works.

How I'd start here

First conversations
01
Find out which partners already run Devin internally. Internal usage predicts real co-sell better than any alliance agreement. It's in your own account data. That should pick the shortlist, not logo size.
02
Educate their engineers before asking anyone to co-market. The Rackspace lesson. Adoption inside the partner comes first, campaigns second. Done the other way round you get a webinar and no pipeline.
03
Build the bundle, then the campaign. Packaged co-marketing the partner team can sell, with mutual success agreed up front. It's also what lets the model travel to Madrid without being rebuilt.
04
Get deal registration working before any of it runs. Without it, and without separate definitions of partner-sourced and partner-influenced, partner isn't a measurable channel. It's an anecdote. That's one of your five success criteria and it's settled before the first campaign.
